VoiceFlow 0.2.0 is a large update: Windows gains a new interface and nearly everything the Mac app has, meetings now transcribe each side separately, and Telugu, Hindi and mixed speech are handled far better. You still bring your own free Groq API key ([console.groq.com/keys](https://console.groq.com/keys)), and there are still no accounts, servers or analytics.

## Downloads

| System | File |
|---|---|
| macOS 14 or later (Apple silicon and Intel) | `VoiceFlow-0.2.0-macOS.dmg` |
| Windows 10/11, most PCs | `VoiceFlow-0.2.0-Windows-x64.exe` |
| Windows 11 on Arm | `VoiceFlow-0.2.0-Windows-arm64.exe` |

These builds are not notarized by Apple and not code-signed for Windows, so the first launch needs one extra click:

- macOS: open the app once, then System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway.
- Windows: in the SmartScreen dialog, click More info, then Run anyway.

If this build is self-signed (the section at the end says), moving from the ad hoc 0.1.0 counts as a new identity. That one update needs Open Anyway and new Microphone and Accessibility permissions once.

## Security and privacy

- The app now explains what Groq's terms say: the Groq Services Agreement bars training on your API data, and Groq may keep logs for up to 30 days for reliability and abuse. It links to Groq's Data Controls, where you can turn on Zero Data Retention.
- New network destination: the app asks `api.github.com` at launch and once a day whether a new release exists. The request carries nothing about you, and nothing is downloaded. Turn it off in Settings > About.
- New on Windows: up to 120 characters before your cursor are read through UI Automation and sent to Groq with your transcript, as on macOS. They are never read from password fields, password managers or terminals.
- Meetings now upload your microphone and the call separately, and only the parts with speech. With more than one language listed, the same piece of audio can be sent up to three times.
- Meeting chunks that fail to upload stay in memory only, for at most about 20 minutes of audio, and are never written to disk.
- Settings has Copy debug report, for bug reports. It has versions, audio device names (with any owner name removed), result codes and timings. It has no transcripts, window titles or keys, and nothing is sent unless you paste it somewhere.
- Local diagnostics hold metadata about recent sessions only, never what you said or typed.
- macOS releases can be signed with a stable self-signed certificate, so the app keeps its identity across updates.
- The release workflow now fails if private strings appear in the repository.

## Fixes

- Text insertion re-reads the focused field at the moment of pasting, retries for Electron and Chromium apps, and checks that the text arrived. When it can't confirm, you get a one-click Paste, and apps that never expose their text stay silent.
- Dictation and meetings share one microphone engine on both platforms, so one can start or stop without disturbing the other. Microphone stalls and device changes are handled in one place.
- Windows meeting audio follows you when the default speakers or headset change during a call.
- Each session records why it ended, so a failed delivery can be diagnosed from the debug report.
- The onboarding hotkey step updates when you press the hotkey.

## New features

- Windows has a new Avalonia interface, with Home, Meetings, Insights, Dictionary, Snippets, Style, Scratchpad (`Alt + Shift + S`), Settings and Help.
- Windows now reads the text before your cursor, offers to take notes when a call starts, learns words you correct, and adds `@file` tags in Cursor and Windsurf.
- Both apps show a notice when a new release is out, with a link to its page. Nothing is downloaded automatically.
- List any number of languages. Auto-detect is checked against your list, with a second opinion when the text doesn't read as the language it was labelled.
- Hindi has writing choices: Devanagari, Hindi with English words, or Hinglish in English letters.
- On 11 generated test clips (macOS synthetic voices, 2026-11-10), mean word error rate went from 54% to 13% for Telugu and from 60% to 19% for Hindi, and stayed at 10% for English. Real speech will score differently. Details are in `docs/LANGUAGE-PIPELINE.md`.
- Meetings transcribe the two sides separately, drop repeats and echoes, and rewrite Telugu and Hindi in the writing you chose.
- All Groq calls go through one queue that leaves room for dictation during long meetings. Notes fall back to a second model when the first runs out.
- The Meetings page has search across titles, notes and transcripts, renaming of Others, action items you can tick, and notes you can copy as Markdown.
- The Mac app has a new monochrome design with one accent colour, and a Settings > About page.
- Homebrew cask in this repository, a winget manifest draft, and verification steps in the release notes.

## Known limitations

- Dictation needs an internet connection and a Groq key. Audio goes to Groq.
- The builds aren't notarized or code-signed, as described above.
- macOS only: calendar titles for meetings and Settings > Export my data.
- Updates are a notice with a link. You download new versions yourself.
- Before typing, VoiceFlow checks that the same app is in front and the field isn't a password field. It doesn't yet compare the exact field.
- Command Mode with no selection can rewrite the whole field. Undo is offered for 6 seconds.
- Language figures come from generated voices. There are no measured figures for real speakers yet.
- Groq's free tier limits how much you can dictate. Audio usage is counted locally because Groq sent no audio limit headers when this was built, and that count resets when the app restarts.

## Not yet tested on real hardware

The Windows app has passed automated checks on real Windows x64 and Arm runners, including a self-test of the shipped exe. Real-world testing on people's PCs is still early. These need a person at a real machine:

- Holding the hotkey, speaking, and getting text in real Windows apps, with real microphones and keyboards.
- Meeting audio on real calls on Windows, and the offer to take notes when a call starts.
- Text before the cursor, auto-learning and `@file` tags in real Windows apps.
- The rebuilt Mac interface and update notice across real Macs, which have been checked with rendered screenshots and tests.
- Moving from the ad hoc 0.1.0 to a self-signed build, and keeping approval through `brew upgrade`.
